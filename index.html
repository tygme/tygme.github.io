The leak

Why today's AI cannot plug the hole

Every mainstream AI approach learns from a record: documents, tickets, transcripts, past data. Your best people's judgment is exactly what the record leaves out, so it keeps leaking out through retirements while the AI spend goes up.

“Today’s AI” here means chatbots and language models, search over documents (RAG), fine-tuning, AI agents, and interview-based rule systems. Each learns from a record, and your best people’s judgment is what the record leaves out.

Why it is not an easy task

Watch an expert, and the expert performs for you

This is the Hawthorne effect, named for productivity studies at Western Electric's Hawthorne Plant in the 1920s and early 1930s: people change their behavior when they know they are being watched. Those original experiments have since been heavily criticized, and researchers now prefer the term "research participation effects," but the practical warning stands. Observation changes behavior, and interviews fare no better, because asked how they work, experts describe the textbook.

An old problem

Plato saw it 2,400 years ago

In the Meno, a student asks how anyone can search for something they do not already know. Socrates answers by drawing geometry out of an untaught boy with questions alone. The puzzle has never gone away. His way through: do not ask the expert to explain. Put real cases in front of the expert, let the expert judge them, and learn from the corrections. His dissertation compared what experts said with what they did.

A defined engagement

Expert Knowledge Capture, in four weeks

Today's AI agents are fluent, but they know nothing of your senior people's judgment. A model of an expert's causes, effects and real episodes is the structured expertise an agent can be grounded on, and it stays with the organization when the expert retires. This is the patented MindModeler method, delivered as a fixed four-week proof of concept.

See the sprint: deliverables, fit and questions Try a working model of an expert
  1. Week 1Interview the expert. Build the semantic model: what they control, what they cannot, and how each factor moves the others.
  2. Week 2The expert authenticates the model and starts using it for "what if" questions.
  3. Week 3Build the episodic model from real episodes: the times it went right, and wrong.
  4. Week 4Validate both models with the expert. Report findings and the path to training, procedures and AI tools.
